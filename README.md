# skils

*(yes, one L. it's a brand now.)*

A loose collection of Claude skills, assembled with the same energy as a
four-track demo recorded in a shared house off Alberta: earnest, slightly
warbly, and shipped before anyone could talk me out of it.

Every commit here is a POOOSH. The first one was `initial POOOSH`. This is
the way.

## What's in the crate

| Skill | What it does | Vibe |
|-------|--------------|------|
| [`pitchfork-2010-pdx`](plugins/pitchfork-2010-pdx/skills/pitchfork-2010-pdx/SKILL.md) | Turns Claude into a Portland-based Pitchfork contributor in 2010 whose entire critical identity is chillwave. Decimal scores. Best New Music calls. Feelings about Washed Out. | sun-bleached, VHS-soft, faintly defensive |

More to come whenever the promo pile gets interesting.

## How to use one

Grab the folder for the skill you want and drop it wherever your Claude
setup looks for skills. Each one is just a `SKILL.md` with frontmatter on
top and personality underneath. No build step. No dependencies. Just
vibes and a Juno-60 nobody plays enough.

If you want the packaged `.skill` version, zip the folder. That's it.
That's the whole packaging pipeline.

## Contributing

Open a PR. Bonus points if the commit message is in all caps and has at
least three O's in it. Extra bonus points if the skill has a strong
opinion about something nobody asked about.

There is a particular kind of light that only exists in Portland in late
September, when the summer has technically ended but nobody has told the
sky yet. It comes in low over the west hills around six, hits the wet
pavement on Division, and for about forty minutes everything looks like it
was shot on a camcorder in 1991. I have been trying for years to explain
to people back east why so much of the music I love sounds like that light
looks, and I have mostly failed, and then this repository showed up in my
inbox with a commit message that read, in its entirety, `initial POOOSH`,
and I thought: oh. There it is. That's the whole thing.

*skils* is a debut in the truest, most bedroom-produced sense. One
contributor. One skill. One misspelled title that the artist has, with
admirable nerve, declined to correct, choosing instead to rebrand the typo
as intent. This is the same move Ariel Pink made when he decided the tape
hiss stayed in the mix, the same move Ernest Greene made when he decided
his vocals should sit so far back they were basically weather. The flaw is
the aesthetic. The aesthetic is the flaw. You either get it or you file a
very confused pull request.

The record, such as it is, opens with the title track. `pitchfork-2010-pdx`
is ninety-four lines of YAML frontmatter and prose, and it is doing
something I have not seen a skill file attempt before, which is to be a
*character study*. Most skills read like appliance manuals: do this, avoid
that, return JSON. This one reads like a Rob Sheffield essay that got lost
on the way to Hipster Runoff and decided to stay. It has a canon. It has a
bike with fenders. It knows the difference between Doug Fir and Holocene
and it has feelings about which one you should be at on a Thursday. It
builds, over the course of a few hundred words, a person who is faintly
aware that they are a type, and then it hands you that person and says:
here, talk to him. The line about chillwave being "the most honest sound of
the recession" lands somewhere around the 2:40 mark of the file, and it
hits the way a sidechain hits, the way the whole mix ducks for a beat and
then swells back louder than it was.

What I keep coming back to, though, is the *restraint*. The persona is
built to tilt hard toward its beat, and the file says so, and then the file
spends a full paragraph making sure the tilt never bends the arithmetic.
"Being the genre's champion makes you harder on bad chillwave, not softer."
That is a craft note, sure, but it is also a thesis about criticism, and it
is more honest than most of what runs on the front page. Somewhere in a
shared house off Alberta, a Juno-60 sits unplayed, and a writer who owns it
has figured out that loving something means being willing to take it apart
in public. That is the most Portland sentence I have ever typed and I stand
by it.

Then there's the README, which functions here as the closing track, the
one that recontextualizes everything before it. It's short. It's a table
and a joke about the letter O. But it does the thing the best closers do,
which is turn the whole record's thesis into a set of instructions for
living: open a PR, commit in all caps, have a strong opinion nobody asked
for. It is "Feel It All Around" as a contributing guide. It is a tweet
about a cassette label that somehow contains a worldview.

One reservation, because a rave without one is just a press release: it is
one file. The table has one row. The word "collection" in the first
paragraph is, at press time, aspirational. There is a real risk that
*skils* becomes one of those Bandcamp pages with a great EP and a "full
length coming soon" note that ages into a small monument. The artist has
the voice; what remains to be seen is whether the artist has the follow-up.
I would not bet against it, but I have been burned by promising cassettes
before.

Still. Debuts are not judged on their tracklists. They are judged on
whether, after the last file closes, you can already hear what the next one
should sound like. I can. It sounds like that six o'clock light on Division,
run through a reverb preset somebody paid actual money for, with a commit
message underneath it that just says POOOSH.
